BMI088 accelerometer and gyro, BMM350 magnetometer, Teensy 4.0 / ESP32 · measured on the real board

Why position cannot be recovered from this IMU

Verdict. Adding an accelerometer's readings up twice gives a position that is wrong by metres within seconds of real movement, and nothing inside the IMU can detect or fix that. The evidence comes in three layers:
  • Maths. A tilt error of 1° is a fake push of 0.17 m/s². It grows into 0.09 m after 1 s, 2.1 m after 5 s and 8.6 m after 10 s. Position is not observable from the IMU alone.
  • Measurements on this board. The sensors are good at rest (tilt known to 0.01°, gyro residual 0.012°/s), yet a board that never moved still "travels" a median 9 cm in 10 s, and 90% of the time up to 39 cm.
  • Real recordings. A slide-left-and-back, hop-left-and-back that ended where it began was tracked 0.94 m away after 25 s, and across eight real sessions one tracker or the other went more than 6 m from the start in four of them, and one reached 36 m.

What is accurate is orientation (roll, pitch, heading). What would be needed for position is a second source of position, such as a camera or UWB.

From accelerometer to position

The accelerometer does not measure acceleration. It measures specific force: gravity and movement mixed together, in the board's own axes.

f_b = Rᵀ (a_e − g_e) + b + n

Here R is the board's orientation, b the sensor's own bias and n noise. To get the movement ae, the code must turn the reading into room axes with its estimate of the orientation, subtract gravity, and integrate twice:

â_e = R̂ (f_b − b̂) + g_e ; v = ∫ â_e dt ; p = ∫ v dt

Every error in R̂ or b̂ lands directly in âe, and integrating twice multiplies it by time squared.

How each error grows

1. A constant accelerometer error

δp = ½ ε t²

2. A tilt error θ (the dominant one)

If the estimated "down" is off by θ, a slice of gravity leaks into the horizontal axes as a constant fake acceleration g sin θ:

δp = ½ g sin θ t² ≈ 0.086 t² m per degree

3. A gyro bias that tilt is not corrected against

A gyro bias bg turns the tilt estimate at a steady rate, θ = bg t, so the error grows with the cube of time:

δp = ⅙ g b_g t³

4. Accelerometer noise

White noise σ per sample at interval Δt becomes a random walk in speed and a faster one in position:

δp_rms = σ √(Δt/3) t^(3/2)

With this board's measured noise (0.026 m/s² per axis at 50 Hz) and gyro residual (0.012°/s) the curves are:

The bar to clear

To hold 1 cm of position error for 10 s, tilt would have to be known to about 0.001°. For 10 cm it is 0.012°. This board's tilt at rest is about 0.010° (median), and it is worse the moment it moves.

Why tilt breaks the moment you move

The board's orientation filter (a Mahony filter in src/main.cpp) uses the accelerometer as its reference for "down". It only rejects a reading when the total is more than 0.15 g away from 1 g. A horizontal push of ah that passes that gate is read partly as tilt, and drags the estimate at a rate set by the filter gain (TWO_KP = 1, so Kp = 0.5):

dθ_error/dt ≈ K_p · a_h / g

A hand move of 1 m/s² for one second therefore leaves about 0.5 × 0.102 rad/s × 1 s ≈ 2.9° of tilt error. That is the "3°" in the chart, and it becomes a sustained fake push of 0.5 m/s² that nothing in the IMU can tell apart from real movement.

The recordings show the accelerometer really does read something other than gravity during movement: at rest the reading is within 0.022 g of 1 g (median); while the board rotates faster than 20°/s it is off by 0.093 g (median) and by 0.51 g at the 95th percentile.

Why no filter can fix it

This is not a tuning problem. With no outside measurement, nothing an accelerometer and gyro sense corrects an error in position, speed or heading: in the standard inertial-navigation error model they are unobservable. Aiding is needed. The most common trick is a zero-velocity update (ZUPT): when the sensor is known to be still, "speed = 0" is fed to a Kalman filter, which then also corrects tilt and bias.

That works for foot-mounted navigation because a foot is planted every step, so the speed error is reset every second or so. Papers on foot-mounted navigation describe it as turning error that grows with the cube of time into error that grows with the number of steps (see the sources below). A hand held in the air has no such rhythm: the recordings contain stretches of 20 s or more of continuous movement with nothing to reset against.

The clearest test needs no measuring rig: move the board and return it to where it started. The true final position is zero.

What this evidence does not show

  • There was no motion-capture or ruler-based ground truth. Where a true answer is stated it is only "the board returned to where it started" or "it never moved".
  • The tilt error during movement is not measured directly (nothing on the board can do that). It is derived from the filter's gain and gate; the 3° figure is an example, not a measurement.
  • One board, one calibration, a few hundred seconds of hand movement. The rest windows were chosen by an automatic threshold.
  • A better sensor or filter would lower every curve by a fixed factor, not by the several-thousand-fold (from about 3° during movement to 0.001°) that the bar above needs.
